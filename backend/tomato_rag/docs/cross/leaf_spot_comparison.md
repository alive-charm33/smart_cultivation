## Quick comparison
| Feature | Early blight | Target spot | Septoria leaf spot | Bacterial spot | Late blight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spot size | Large (up to 1 cm+) | Medium-large | Very small (2-3 mm) | Small (2-3 mm) | Large irregular patches |
| Pattern | Clear concentric rings | Concentric rings, tan centre | Round, gray/tan centre, dark border, tiny black dots | Angular, water-soaked, dark; may look shot-hole | Water-soaked, greasy, brown-black, no rings |
| Halo | Yellow | Yellow (sometimes) | Sometimes slight | Yellow | Pale green edge |
| Where first | Older, lower leaves | Any leaves, including younger | Lower, older leaves | Any leaves | Upper or any leaves; fast |
| Fruit | Stem-end lesions | Pitted spots | Rare | Raised scabby spots | Firm brown greasy blotches |
| Weather | Warm, alternating wet/dry | Warm, humid, dense canopy | Warm, wet, splashing | Warm, wet, windy | Cool, wet, foggy |
| Special sign | Rings | Larger uniform rings | Many tiny spots | Shot-hole, scabby fruit | White fuzz underneath in humid mornings |

## Questions to ask the user when the prediction is uncertain
1. Are the spots on old lower leaves only, or on new leaves as well?
2. Are the spots tiny and numerous, or few and large?
3. Do the spots have rings? Are they water-soaked or dry?
4. Is there white fuzz on the leaf underside? (late blight) Or olive velvet? (leaf mold)
5. Have the last days been cool and foggy, warm and rainy, or hot and dry?
6. Is fruit affected? What does it look like?
7. What is the growth stage?

## Treatment overlap
Early blight, target spot and Septoria share protectant fungicides (mancozeb, chlorothalonil, copper), so protective sprays and sanitation are broadly similar. Bacterial spot needs copper-based products and does not respond to fungicides such as azoxystrobin. Late blight needs a specific oomycete program and urgent action.
