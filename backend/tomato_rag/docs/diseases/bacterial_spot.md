## Symptoms
- Small (about 2-3 mm), **water-soaked, dark, angular to round spots** on leaves, often with a yellow halo; spots may dry and tear, giving a shot-hole look.
- Leaf margins may blacken; severe cases cause leaf drop.
- Small, raised, scabby, brown, corky spots on green fruit (fruit surface only, but marketability is lost).
- Spots on stems, petioles and flowers; flower drop reduces yield.

## Favorable conditions
Warm (24-30 C), humid, rainy or windy weather; overhead irrigation. Spreads by splashing water, wind-driven rain, tools and workers. Seed-borne and survives on debris and volunteer plants.

## Prevention
- Use certified disease-free seed and healthy transplants; buy seedlings only from reputable nurseries.
- Seed treatment as recommended locally (hot water treatment at the recommended temperature and time, or approved seed treatment).
- Rotate away from tomato and chilli/pepper for at least 1-2 years.
- Drip irrigation only; avoid overhead irrigation; avoid working in wet plants.
- Sanitize tools and hands; remove volunteers and weeds.
- Bury or destroy residues after harvest.

## Cultural control when disease is seen
- Remove severely infected plants and bag them.
- Avoid moving between fields with wet foliage; disinfect tools between plants.

## Organic / biological control
- Copper-based bactericides (protective, not curative). Combine with cultural measures.
- Some biological products (e.g., Bacillus-based) suggest partial suppression only.

## Chemical control (verify on CIB&RC; follow label dose, interval and PHI)
- Copper oxychloride or other copper formulations (FRAC M01); commonly used in India together with streptomycin + tetracycline (streptocycline) as per state recommendation.
- Copper-tolerant strains are widespread, so copper alone can fail; mixing with mancozeb is a recognized practice to improve copper activity in some regions.
- Spray protectively in humid, rainy weather from early stage; there is no product that cures infected tissue. Do not overuse antibiotic products; follow local recommendations.

## Stage-wise action
| Stage | Risk | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Nursery | High | Certified seed/seed treatment; no overhead watering; protective spray on seedlings if recommended |
| Transplanting | Medium-High | Discard spotted seedlings; disinfect tools |
| Vegetative | Medium | Drip irrigation; do not work in wet foliage |
| Flowering/fruit set | High | Protective sprays in rain/humidity; avoid touching plants when wet |
| Fruit development | High | Prevent fruit spotting; keep canopy dry |
| Ripening/harvest | Medium | Harvest dry; discard scabby fruit; clean crates |
| End of season | - | Remove debris; rotate 1-2 years |

## Look-alikes
Septoria (round spots with gray centres, no fruit lesions), early blight (large rings). Bacterial spot lesions are small, angular and water-soaked; fruit shows raised scabby spots. See `cross/leaf_spot_comparison.md`.
