## Symptoms
- Pale green to yellow patches with indistinct margins on the **upper leaf surface**.
- **Olive-green to brown velvety mold** on the corresponding **underside** of the leaf (the key diagnostic sign).
- Patches merge; leaves curl, wither and drop. Older leaves are affected first.
- Blossoms may drop; fruit infection is uncommon (dark leathery rot at stem end).

## Favorable conditions
High relative humidity (above ~85%), poor ventilation, moderate temperature (about 22-25 C), and long leaf wetness. Mainly a problem in **polyhouses/greenhouses**, but appears in the field during humid, cloudy or rainy weather in dense canopies.

## Prevention
- Grow resistant varieties/hybrids (Cf gene resistant) where available; note that races of the fungus can overcome single genes.
- Reduce humidity: ventilate polyhouses, use drip irrigation, avoid night-time leaf wetness.
- Wider spacing, staking, and removal of lower leaves for airflow.
- Avoid excess nitrogen, which increases canopy density.
- Sanitize tools and structures between crops; destroy residues.

## Cultural control when disease is seen
- Remove infected leaves; increase ventilation and lower humidity immediately.
- Water in the morning at the base only.

## Organic / biological control
- Copper-based sprays (protectant) and Bacillus-based biofungicides can slow spread; the main control is humidity management.

## Chemical control (verify on CIB&RC; follow label dose, interval and PHI)
- Protectants: chlorothalonil (FRAC M05), mancozeb (M03), copper oxychloride (M01).
- Systemic options where registered: difenoconazole (FRAC 3), azoxystrobin (FRAC 11).
- Spray at first symptoms or before, when humid weather is forecast; ensure coverage of leaf undersides; rotate modes of action.

## Stage-wise action
| Stage | Risk | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Nursery | Low | Ventilate trays and nursery house |
| Transplanting | Low | Wide spacing; choose resistant hybrids |
| Vegetative | Medium | Prune lower leaves; ventilate; balanced N |
| Flowering/fruit set | Medium-High | Control humidity; scout leaf undersides |
| Fruit development | High | Dense canopy = high risk; maintain airflow and spray if needed |
| Ripening/harvest | Medium | Observe PHI; keep canopy open |
| End of season | - | Clean polyhouse and residues |

## Look-alikes
Early nutrient deficiency yellowing (no mold underneath). Always check the leaf underside for velvety olive mold.
