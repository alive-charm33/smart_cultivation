## Symptoms
- Light and dark green **mosaic mottling** on leaves; leaflets may be distorted, narrowed (fern-leaf/shoestring), or blistered.
- Stunted growth; reduced fruit set; fruit may ripen unevenly with internal browning.
- Symptoms are worse in cool weather and low light and may be masked in hot weather.

## Key facts
- **No chemical treatment exists.** Sprays do not cure infected plants.
- The virus is extremely stable: it spreads by **contaminated hands, clothes, tools, stakes, and plant sap**, through seed contamination, and on crop debris and soil for a long time.
- Tobacco users can carry related viruses on their hands; wash hands before handling plants.
- Not spread significantly by whitefly (unlike leaf curl virus).

## Prevention
- Use **certified virus-free seed**; seed treatment (soaking in trisodium phosphate solution, or dry-heat/hot water treatment as recommended locally) reduces seed-borne virus.
- Use **resistant varieties/hybrids** (Tm-1, Tm-2, Tm-2^2 gene resistance) where available.
- Wash hands with soap and water before handling plants; do not smoke or use tobacco near plants.
- Disinfect tools and stakes (e.g., with a suitable disinfectant such as bleach solution or milk-soap wash as locally advised) between plants and fields.
- Do not reuse infected stakes, trays or plastic sheets without disinfection.
- Rotate crops and remove residues, roots and weeds; avoid planting tomato in fields where infected crop grew recently.
- Work on healthy plants first and infected areas last; avoid handling plants when wet.

## What to do when infected plants are seen
- Remove infected plants immediately (with roots), bag and destroy them; do not compost.
- Wash hands and disinfect tools before touching another plant.
- Reduce pruning and handling of the remaining plants where possible.

## Organic / biological control
- None effective on infected plants; **sanitation and resistant varieties are the only reliable control**.

## Chemical control
- No antiviral chemical available for growers. Do not spray fungicides or insecticides expecting a cure. (Vector sprays are not relevant for ToMV.)

## Stage-wise action
| Stage | Risk | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Nursery | High | Virus-free seed and treatment; clean trays; workers wash hands |
| Transplanting | High | Reject mottled seedlings; disinfect hands and tools |
| Vegetative | High | Limit handling; rogue infected plants; sanitize tools |
| Flowering/fruit set | Medium-High | Disinfect tools during pruning/staking; handle healthy first |
| Fruit development | Medium | Same sanitation; avoid working when wet |
| Ripening/harvest | Medium | Wash hands and crates; remove infected plants |
| End of season | - | Remove all roots/debris; disinfect stakes and structures |

## Look-alikes
Leaf curl virus (upward curl, yellowing, whitefly, no mottling), herbicide injury, nutrient deficiency. Mosaic shows a patchy light/dark green pattern.
