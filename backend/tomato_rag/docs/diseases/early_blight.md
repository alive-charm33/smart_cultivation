## Symptoms
- Brown to dark-brown spots with **concentric rings** (target/bullseye pattern) surrounded by a yellow halo, on **older, lower leaves first**.
- Spots enlarge and merge; badly affected leaves yellow and drop, exposing fruit to sunscald.
- Dark, sunken, ringed lesions on stems (collar rot at seedling base in nursery).
- Dark, leathery, sunken lesions with concentric rings at the stem end of fruit.

## Favorable conditions
Warm temperature (about 24-29 C), alternating wet and dry spells, heavy dew, high humidity, plant stress (nitrogen or potassium deficiency, heavy fruit load). Fungus survives in crop debris, soil and on seed.

## Prevention
- Use certified disease-free seed; treat seed as per local recommendation.
- Rotate with non-solanaceous crops (cereals, legumes) for 2-3 years; avoid potato/brinjal/chilli before tomato.
- Balanced fertilization: avoid nitrogen or potassium starvation, which makes plants more susceptible.
- Stake and prune for airflow; mulch to reduce soil splash.
- Drip irrigation; avoid wetting leaves.
- Remove crop debris and weeds after harvest.

## Cultural control when disease is seen
- Remove the lowest infected leaves as soon as spots appear; discard away from the field.
- Do not handle plants when foliage is wet.

## Organic / biological control
- Copper-based sprays, Bacillus subtilis or Trichoderma/Pseudomonas fluorescens formulations give partial protection when applied early.
- Neem-based products have limited effect; use only as a supplement.

## Chemical control (verify registration on CIB&RC; follow label dose, interval and PHI)
- Protectants: mancozeb (FRAC M03), chlorothalonil (M05), copper oxychloride (M01).
- Systemic/translaminar: azoxystrobin (FRAC 11), difenoconazole (FRAC 3), azoxystrobin + difenoconazole, pyraclostrobin-based mixtures where registered.
- Start at first symptoms or when weather is favorable; repeat at label interval. Alternate modes of action to slow resistance (QoI resistance is documented in Alternaria).

## Stage-wise action
| Stage | Risk | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Nursery | Medium | Use clean seed/trays; watch for stem lesions and collar rot; ventilate |
| Transplanting | Medium | Reject plants with stem lesions; avoid deep planting in infested soil |
| Vegetative | Medium | Balanced nutrition; mulch; remove lowest infected leaves |
| Flowering/fruit set | Medium-High | Start protectant sprays if lesions are present; keep foliage dry |
| Fruit development | High | Heavy fruit load raises risk; maintain nutrition and spray program |
| Ripening/harvest | High | Observe PHI; remove diseased fruit; avoid harvesting wet |
| End of season | - | Remove/destroy debris; rotate field |

## Look-alikes
Target spot (larger, more uniform rings, can affect all leaves), Septoria (many tiny circular spots with dark border and gray centre), bacterial spot (small water-soaked angular spots). See `cross/leaf_spot_comparison.md`. Model note: early blight is the class most often misclassified as another brown-spot disease.

## When to seek expert help
If more than about a third of leaf area is lost before fruit set, or fruit lesions appear.
