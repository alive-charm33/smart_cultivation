## Symptoms
- Tiny yellow or white stippling on the upper leaf surface; leaves turn bronze/yellow, dry and drop.
- Fine webbing on the underside of leaves and between shoots in heavy infestations.
- Tiny (about 0.5 mm) moving mites, pale/greenish with two dark spots, visible on the leaf underside with a hand lens.
- Infestations begin in patches, often at field edges and dusty areas, and spread rapidly.

## Favorable conditions
Hot, dry weather (above about 30 C), dust, water stress and drought, and misuse of broad-spectrum insecticides (which kill natural enemies). Populations can double in a few days in hot weather. Weeds and neighbouring crops (okra, brinjal, cotton, beans) act as reservoirs.

## Prevention
- Keep plants well irrigated and avoid drought stress; reduce dust near crops.
- Control weeds in and around the field.
- Avoid unnecessary broad-spectrum insecticides (especially synthetic pyrethroids and some organophosphates), which flare mites by killing predators.
- Check leaf undersides weekly from transplanting onward, especially at field edges.
- Do not carry mites in on seedlings: inspect nursery plants.

## Cultural control when infested
- Remove and destroy heavily infested leaves and plants.
- Overhead water spray can reduce mite numbers temporarily but may raise fungal disease risk, so use with care.

## Organic / biological control
- Predatory mites (Phytoseiulus persimilis, Neoseiulus californicus) and lady beetles such as Stethorus.
- Neem oil, soap or horticultural oil sprays and sulphur-based products (avoid in hot weather and on sensitive stages), with thorough underside coverage.

## Chemical control (verify on CIB&RC; follow label dose, interval and PHI)
- Miticides commonly used in tomato in India include abamectin (IRAC 6), spiromesifen (IRAC 23), fenpyroximate (IRAC 21A), propargite (IRAC 12C), and sulphur formulations.
- Rotate IRAC groups between sprays; mites develop resistance quickly. Do not use the same group repeatedly in one season.
- Thorough coverage of leaf undersides is essential; observe pre-harvest intervals, since fruit is picked continuously.

## Stage-wise action
| Stage | Risk | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Nursery | Low | Inspect for mites; keep clean of weeds |
| Transplanting | Low-Medium | Do not plant infested seedlings; check field edges |
| Vegetative | Medium | Weekly scouting; avoid stress; protect predators |
| Flowering/fruit set | High | Act at first hot spots; use selective miticide or oil |
| Fruit development | High | Rotate miticide groups; observe PHI |
| Ripening/harvest | Medium-High | Only products with short PHI; clean picking tools |
| End of season | - | Destroy residues and weeds |

## Look-alikes
Nutrient deficiency, sun scorch, and herbicide drift can look similar; webbing and mites on the underside confirm spider mites. Whitefly-transmitted virus gives curling and yellowing but not stippling or webbing.
