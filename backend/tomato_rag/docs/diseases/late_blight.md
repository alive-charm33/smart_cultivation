## Symptoms
- Large, irregular, water-soaked greasy patches on leaves that turn brown to black, often starting at leaf edges or tips.
- White, fuzzy sporulation on the underside of leaves at the edge of lesions in humid, cool mornings.
- Dark brown-black lesions on stems and petioles; whole plant can collapse within days.
- Firm, brown, greasy-looking blotches on green and ripening fruit.

## Favorable conditions
Cool nights (about 10-20 C), warm-mild days, leaf wetness, fog, drizzle, and relative humidity above ~90%. In Maharashtra it is a concern during monsoon and cool, cloudy winter spells (high-altitude and cool-region plantings are at highest risk). Spreads very fast by wind-blown spores.

## Prevention
- Use healthy, disease-free seedlings; never transplant plants with lesions.
- Avoid planting next to potato fields or near infected volunteer potato/tomato plants.
- Choose well-drained fields; use wide spacing, staking and pruning for airflow.
- Drip irrigation instead of overhead sprinklers; irrigate in the morning so foliage dries quickly.
- Remove and destroy volunteer plants and crop debris at season end.
- Grow tolerant/resistant hybrids where available (Ph-2/Ph-3 gene hybrids).

## Cultural control when disease is seen
- Remove and bury or burn infected plants/leaves (bag them; do not compost).
- Stop overhead watering; improve ventilation in polyhouses.
- Scout every 2-3 days in cool wet weather.

## Organic / biological control
- Copper-based products (protectant only) can slow spread in organic systems.
- Bio-agents such as Bacillus subtilis or Trichoderma-based products are supportive only; they do not stop an active epidemic.

## Chemical control (verify registration on CIB&RC; follow label dose, interval and pre-harvest interval)
- Protectant (start before symptoms in risk weather): mancozeb (FRAC M03), chlorothalonil (M05), copper oxychloride (M01).
- Curative/systemic partners (rotate, do not repeat the same group consecutively): cymoxanil + mancozeb, metalaxyl + mancozeb (FRAC 4; resistance is common, use limited sprays), dimethomorph (FRAC 40), famoxadone + cymoxanil, fenamidone + mancozeb, azoxystrobin (FRAC 11).
- Rotate modes of action and mix a protectant with a systemic. Spray when weather is favorable, not only after symptoms appear. Respect pre-harvest intervals on fruit.

## Stage-wise action
| Stage | Risk | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Nursery | Low-Medium | Keep nursery ventilated, avoid wet foliage overnight; inspect daily |
| Transplanting | Medium | Plant only healthy seedlings; well-drained beds |
| Vegetative | High | Protectant spray program in cool, wet weather; prune lower leaves |
| Flowering/fruit set | High | Continue protectant; avoid overhead irrigation |
| Fruit development | Very high | Protect fruit and canopy; remove infected plants early |
| Ripening/harvest | High | Observe pre-harvest interval; do not pick wet fruit; discard lesioned fruit |
| End of season | - | Destroy residues; do not leave volunteers |

## Look-alikes
Early blight (has concentric rings, yellow halo, starts on older leaves) and bacterial diseases (small angular spots). See `cross/leaf_spot_comparison.md`.

## When to seek expert help
Rapid spread across the field within 2-3 days, stem lesions, or fruit infection: contact the local Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) or agriculture officer.
