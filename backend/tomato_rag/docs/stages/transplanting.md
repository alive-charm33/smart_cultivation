## Timing
Typically the first 1-2 weeks after transplanting (0-14 days after transplanting, DAT). Timing varies with variety, hybrid, season and temperature. Indeterminate hybrids flower, set fruit and ripen fruit at the same time, so more than one stage can apply. If the day count and what you see on the plant disagree, trust the plant.

## What happens
Seedlings are moved to the field, experience transplant shock, and develop new roots. Plants are small and susceptible to virus and stress.

## Water and nutrient needs
Irrigate immediately after planting and keep moisture even. Apply basal fertilizer (as per soil test) with organic manure. A phosphorus-rich starter helps root growth. Avoid heavy nitrogen at this point.

## Key management
- Plant only healthy, hardened seedlings; avoid deep planting that buries the stem in wet soil.
- Well-drained beds, drip lines, and (optionally) silver/reflective plastic mulch to repel whiteflies.
- Border crop such as maize or sorghum can reduce whitefly entry.
- Field sanitation and rotation from previous solanaceous crops.
- Begin weekly scouting and yellow sticky traps.

## Disease and pest risks at this stage
| Disease | Risk | Note |
|---|---|---|
| tylcv | Very high | Infections in the first 50-60 days cause greatest loss |
| tomato_mosaic_virus | High | Contaminated hands and tools during planting |
| bacterial_spot | Medium-High | Carried on seedlings; splashing water |
| early_blight | Medium | Lesions on stem base of seedlings |
| late_blight | Medium | If cool and wet after planting |
| spider_mites | Low-Medium | Hot, dry, dusty conditions |
