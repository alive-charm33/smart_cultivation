## Timing
Typically about 45-70 days after transplanting (DAT). New trusses keep flowering while lower fruit is enlarging. Timing varies with variety, hybrid, season and temperature. Indeterminate hybrids flower, set fruit and ripen fruit at the same time, so more than one stage can apply. If the day count and what you see on the plant disagree, trust the plant.

## What happens
Fruits enlarge and fill. The plant carries a heavy fruit load, so nutrient and water demands are highest, and stress lowers resistance to early blight.

## Water and nutrient needs
Steady irrigation; irregular watering causes cracking and blossom-end rot. High potassium and adequate calcium; moderate nitrogen. Maintain nutrition to prevent stress-related early blight.

## Key management
- Keep a protective/curative program based on weather and scouting.
- Remove infected leaves and fruit; keep canopy open but do not expose fruit to sunscald.
- Watch **pre-harvest intervals (PHI)** for every product used, because picking may start soon.
- Rotate fungicide and miticide modes of action.

## Disease and pest risks at this stage
| Disease | Risk | Note |
|---|---|---|
| early_blight | High | Heavy fruit load and older leaves |
| late_blight | Very high | Affects fruit and canopy in wet cool weather |
| septoria_leaf_spot | High | Defoliation exposes fruit to sunscald |
| target_spot | High | Fruit and leaf lesions in humid canopy |
| leaf_mold | High | Dense canopy in humid weather |
| bacterial_spot | High | Fruit scabs and leaf spots |
| spider_mites | High | Hot dry weather |
