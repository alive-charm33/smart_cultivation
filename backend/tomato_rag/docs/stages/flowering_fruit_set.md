## Timing
Typically about 30-50 days after transplanting (DAT). It overlaps with vegetative growth (until about day 35) and fruit development (from about day 45). Timing varies with variety, hybrid, season and temperature. Indeterminate hybrids flower, set fruit and ripen fruit at the same time, so more than one stage can apply. If the day count and what you see on the plant disagree, trust the plant.

## What happens
Flower trusses open and pollination and fruit set occur. Flower drop is the main yield risk. Plants are sensitive to heat, cold, humidity extremes and water stress.

## Water and nutrient needs
Consistent moisture; avoid both drought and flooding. Shift emphasis from nitrogen to potassium; ensure calcium and boron are adequate for fruit set. Excess nitrogen delays flowering and raises disease.

## Key management
- Maintain a regular protective program for leaf diseases based on weather.
- Avoid overhead irrigation; do not handle plants when wet.
- Prune to keep the canopy open; remove diseased lower leaves.
- Continue whitefly and mite monitoring; avoid pesticides toxic to pollinators in the middle of the day, and follow label pollinator precautions.
- Septoria and bacterial spot often become serious from first fruit set onward.

## Disease and pest risks at this stage
| Disease | Risk | Note |
|---|---|---|
| bacterial_spot | High | Flower and fruit infection; rain splash |
| septoria_leaf_spot | High | Escalates after first fruit set |
| early_blight | Medium-High | Rising with fruit load and stress |
| late_blight | High | If cool, wet weather |
| leaf_mold | Medium-High | Humid dense canopy |
| target_spot | Medium-High | Humid canopy |
| spider_mites | High | Hot, dry weather |
| tylcv | Medium | Late infections cause less loss but plants remain a source |
