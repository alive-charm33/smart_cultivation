## Timing
Starts after the final harvest, usually 105 or more days after transplanting (DAT), earlier for determinate varieties. Timing varies with variety, hybrid, season and temperature. Indeterminate hybrids flower, set fruit and ripen fruit at the same time, so more than one stage can apply. If the day count and what you see on the plant disagree, trust the plant.

## What happens
The crop declines after final harvest. Old plants become a reservoir of pathogens, pests and viruses for the next crop.

## Key management
- Remove plants **with roots**; bag, bury or burn diseased material (do not compost diseased residues).
- Deep-plough or bury residues; manage volunteers and weeds (solanaceous weeds especially).
- Disinfect stakes, trays, plastic sheets, crates and tools (essential for tomato mosaic virus).
- Leave a crop-free gap and rotate with non-solanaceous crops for 2-3 years.
- Do not leave old plants near a new nursery or a new field (whiteflies move to new plants).
- Consider soil solarization in the hot season for fields with heavy disease history.

## Diseases that carry over
early_blight, septoria_leaf_spot, target_spot and bacterial_spot survive on debris; tomato_mosaic_virus survives on debris and tools; tylcv survives in living weeds and other crops; spider_mites survive on weeds and in residues.
