## Timing
Typically about 15-35 days after transplanting (DAT). Flowering begins to overlap from about day 30. Timing varies with variety, hybrid, season and temperature. Indeterminate hybrids flower, set fruit and ripen fruit at the same time, so more than one stage can apply. If the day count and what you see on the plant disagree, trust the plant.

## What happens
Rapid leaf, stem and side-shoot growth; staking and pruning begin. Canopy thickens, raising humidity inside the crop.

## Water and nutrient needs
Regular even irrigation (drip). Nitrogen is important now but avoid excess, which increases lush, disease-prone canopy. Apply nutrients in splits; include potassium and calcium as the crop moves toward flowering.

## Key management
- Stake and tie plants; remove suckers and lowest leaves for airflow.
- Mulch to reduce soil splash of early blight and Septoria.
- Weekly scouting (upper and lower leaf surface).
- Start protective fungicide program if late blight risk weather (cool, wet) is present.
- Sanitize tools between plants during pruning; handle healthy plants first.

## Disease and pest risks at this stage
| Disease | Risk | Note |
|---|---|---|
| late_blight | High | Cool, wet weather; very fast spread |
| early_blight | Medium | Lower leaves; spread by splashing |
| leaf_mold | Medium | Dense canopy, high humidity, polyhouses |
| target_spot | Medium | Any leaves in humid canopy |
| septoria_leaf_spot | Medium | Wet, warm weather; lower leaves |
| spider_mites | Medium | Hot, dry weather; check undersides |
| tylcv | High | Continued whitefly pressure; rogue infected plants |
| tomato_mosaic_virus | High | Spread by pruning and tying |
| bacterial_spot | Medium | Rain and wind splash |
